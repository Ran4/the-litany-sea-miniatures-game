## The world in one breath

A god named **Veyl** was struck down in the deep of years. It did not bleed — it **wept**,
and its weeping filled the basin of the world. That ocean of grief is the **Litany Sea**,
and every drop of brine holds a remembered moment. Now the Sea is **drying**. A **rust-red
sun has not set in living memory**; the water recedes shin-deep, then ankle-deep, then gone,
and where it goes it **crystallizes into salt-glass** — each shard a fossilized instant you
can re-live, sell, **set forever**, or **shatter so it never happened at all**.

**The twist (the deepest truth):** Veyl is **not dead — it is grieving**, and the Sea is not
a corpse but an open, weeping **wound, still dreaming**. Every crystal is a tear it hasn't
finished crying. The "history" the orders fight over is the god *re-remembering its own life
as it dies*. The **salt-dead rise** because the god is flinching at moments it cannot let go.
When the last pool dries, Veyl finishes the thought — and **whichever version of the past is
crystallized at that instant becomes the only past that ever was.** The orders think they are
scavengers looting a divine cadaver. They are the competing voices inside a dying mind's last
sentence. The loser's past does not just fail to score — **it ceases ever to have happened.**

## Key terms (use these words)

- **The Long Toll** — the shared, audible doomsday countdown of the receding brine; measured
  in **tides** (rounds). Everyone hears the same clock. When it strikes **Dry**, the game ends.
- **Salt-glass / a Litany** — a crystallized memory. A *named*, lore-bearing crystal is a
  **Litany**; common ones are just salt-glass. Fragile, irreplaceable, **humming**.
- **Brine-deep / Dry** — the two states of any zone. **Brine-deep** (wet) zones slow you but
  **cushion crystals** (auto-pass shatter). **Dry** zones are fast and lethal; a crystal there
  is **Exposed** and can shatter from stray fire.
- **Setting** — making a crystal **permanent**, exactly as it happened, so it can never be
  edited, sold, shattered, moved, or re-dreamed. **The Mnemarchs' sacred verb and win path.**
- **The Verdict** — the end scoring: which faction's *version of the truth* survived intact.
- **Iodine-blind** — slang for an archivist who has re-lived so many crystals they can no
  longer tell their own memories from the Sea's. The **smell of iodine = a memory crystallizing
  nearby.**
- **Reliquiae / the salt-dead** — drowned-grey ghosts of forgotten moments, half-risen from the
  crust, re-remembering how to walk wherever the tide abandons them.
- **The wake** — running through brine makes a wake; a wake is a hammer; the prize is glass.
  So you **wade, you do not run.** ("A kill costs you nothing here. A wake costs you everything.")

## The four orders (and how each *answers the same question*: what is the past FOR?)

- **The Mnemarchs (Order of the Set Hour)** — the past is sacred and must be **FIXED**. *Our faction.*
- **The Ash Iconoclasts (the Shattering)** — the past is a wound; **ERASE** it. They shatter
  Litanies the others die to protect. Charcoal & ember-orange; mallets; zealots.
- **The Brine-Factors (the Salt Exchange)** — the past is inventory; **SELL** it. Fast skiff-riders,
  cutpurses, editors of memory. Brass & teal.
- **The Sediment (the Re-Dreaming)** — the past should be **FREED**; the salt-dead swarm, grief
  given a face. They re-dream the dead back into play.

## THE MNEMARCHS — deep canon

**Battle cry:** *"What was, shall be. We do not remember it — we make it so."*
**Color:** bone-white & verdigris bronze — bleached vestments, oxidized reliquary fittings,
salt-rimed gold.
**Identity:** cathedral-archivists in **salt-stiffened vestments** who believe the past must be
made permanent, true, and untouchable. They do not loot history; they **enshrine** it. To **Set**
a memory in salt-glass forever, exactly as it happened, is their highest sacrament and win condition.
**Playstyle (must feel true in the story):** elite, few, expensive, durable, **defensive**. They
**bunker around a single Litany in the deepest pool** and **grind out the clock**, daring you to
come take an objective you cannot safely shoot near.

### Origin — Osseil the Stilled
The Order began with one wading hermit at the **First Recession**: an old reliquary-keeper named
**Osseil the Stilled**, the first to re-live a crystal. Where others felt the *thrill* that the
past could be **changed**, Osseil felt **horror**: if the past can be edited, nothing is true, and
a world with no fixed truth is already dead. Osseil knelt in the shrinking margin and did the
opposite of editing — pressed both palms to a crystallizing tear and **willed it to stay exactly
as it was, forever**, refusing the temptation to make it better. The crystal **Set**. It rang once,
clear, and could never again be altered, sold, or broken. Osseil wept, understanding this was the
only mercy the dying Sea could be given **and the only blasphemy**: to seal a moment of a god's
grief and call it scripture. From that **first Set Hour** grew the Order.

### Culture
Liturgical, slow, obsessively rigorous. Vestments **crust harder with every crystal you Set**, so
a senior archivist can barely move — **rigidity is rank**; a fully-Set elder hasn't bent at the
waist in years. Nothing is true until **two witnesses re-live a crystal and swear it matches, word
for word, breath for breath** — they speak in **attestations**. Editing is heresy, selling is
desecration; the deepest sin is the Iconoclast's — to unmake a moment is to **murder a piece of the
divine**. They keep the **Shattering of the Concord Litany** (the truce-crystal all four had agreed
to protect, broken in crossfire — now no one remembers agreeing to peace) as their **original wound**,
and recite the names of moments that "never happened" as a **funeral rite**, mourning histories no
one else can even remember losing. They believe they are **the Sea's confessors**, taking down its
last words exactly, so that when Veyl dies it dies **remembered truly** rather than flattered, sold,
or silenced.

### Why they fight
The Verdict is coming and they alone believe the past has intrinsic worth that must not be touched.
If the Brine-Factors win, the god's last memory is a forgery. If the Iconoclasts win, there is no
last memory at all. If the Sediment wins, every fixed truth dissolves. They fight to **Set the true
moments before the Toll strikes dry**, so Veyl carries into death an honest account of its own life.
They don't want the *most* history — they want the **right** history to survive untouched, and they
will **bunker around one sacred Litany and grind out the entire clock**.

### Leadership & ranks (lived hierarchy)
- **Set Hierarch** — currently **Antiphon Vael, the Unbending**, so thoroughly Set into salt-vestment
  they can **no longer walk**; carried into battle on a **reliquary palanquin** of crystals they
  sealed decades ago; speaks only in confirmed attestations. Slow as a glacier; the last thing
  standing in the dry.
- **The Two Witnesses (Concord & Antiphony)** — paired officers who **must both live and both agree**
  for any truth to be Set; never separate by more than a few paces; finish one another's attestations.
  **Kill one and the Order's sacrament freezes** until a new pair is sworn — a fragility they consider
  sacred, not foolish.
- **Lectors of the Permitted Truth** — recite the day's allowable truths aloud, and re-live crystals
  to attest them, steadily going **iodine-blind** on the Sea's edge.

### Homeland — the Set Hour
They claim **no ground** (ground is not history). Their seat is **the Set Hour** itself: a vast
**cathedral built entirely from crystals they have Set**, hauled inward from the receding margins
over generations, stacked into **vaulted naves of salt-glass that ring like bells when the wind
crosses them**. It is **mobile like a glacier**, dragged tide by tide toward the last pools, always
at the drying edge where new truth crystallizes. To stand inside it is to be surrounded by sealed,
unbreakable moments of the world — a building made of remembered time that will be **the last
structure standing when the Sea dries.**

### Units (lived roles — weave these in as people the recruit knows/becomes)
- **Attesting Acolytes** — junior archivists in fresh, still-flexible vestments; wade out, **lay
  hands on a crystal** so a Witness can later Set it. They can **Hold** but not Set. The cheapest,
  most expendable thing the Order admits to owning — **and even they refuse to run.** They **die
  holding the Litany** rather than let go.
- **Chime-Wardens** — still-standing marksmen who fire only **sworn, attested shots** and make the
  approach to a guarded pool cost bodies. (Weapon: **attestation longgun**.)
- **Verdigris Wardens** — bronze-crusted veterans so oxidized they **ring green when struck**; plant
  in the deepest pool and **become terrain**. You don't push them off; you wait for the water to
  leave, and they'll still be there. (Weapon: **reliquary maul**.)
- **The Sealing Choir** — robed singers who perform the **Set Hour as liturgy**, racing the drying
  Toll, gambling on a single die that the moment **Sets rather than shatters**. (Subfaction: the
  **Set Choir** will **Set under fire, in the open**, racing the Toll rather than waiting for safety.)
- **Pilgrim-Bearers of the Sealed Vault** — stooped bearers hauling **one fixed truth out of the
  crossfire**, defenseless and utterly devoted, each a walking reliquary.
- **Reliquary Sentinels** — immobile gun-shrines bolted into the pool floor; **one heavy attested
  shot** punishes the deep-water approach. (Weapon: **toll-organ harpoon**.)

### Signature mechanic — **The Set Hour** (must surface as lived ritual, not rules)
Once per round, a Mnemarch in contact with a **Held** crystal **in a Brine-deep zone** may **Set**
it: it can **never** again be held, edited, sold, shattered, moved, or re-dreamed — permanent, and
it counts for the Mnemarch Verdict forever. **The only way to make a crystal truly permanent.** The
agony: **Set too early** and you waste the sacrament on something nobody contests; **wait for the
wet zone to be safe** and the **Toll may dry it out from under you first.** You win not by holding
the most crystals at the end, but by **how many you Set before the water left.**

Faction doctrines (texture for the prose):
- **Salt-Stiffened** — you are crusted into your vestments; you **auto-pass any forced move/push/
  fall-back** (too rigid to be shoved) but you can **never Run, ever.** You hold exactly where you
  stand. An anvil that cannot be moved and cannot chase.
- **Confessor's Patience** — if you did not move all round, your attested shot **re-rolls every
  miss.** Stillness is rewarded with re-rolls, never bonuses.
- **Cushion of the Brine** — a crystal in a Brine-deep zone **auto-passes every shatter check** (the
  water protects it). The instant the Toll flips that zone Dry, the cushion is gone.
