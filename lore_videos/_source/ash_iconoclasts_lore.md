## The world in one breath (shared canon)

A god named **Veyl** was struck down in the deep of years. It did not bleed — it **wept**,
and its weeping filled the basin of the world. That ocean of grief is the **Litany Sea**, and
every drop of brine holds a remembered moment. Now the Sea is **drying**. A **rust-red sun has
not set in living memory**; the water recedes shin-deep, then ankle-deep, then gone, and where
it goes it **crystallizes into salt-glass** — each shard a fossilized instant you can re-live,
sell, set forever, or **shatter so it never happened at all**.

**The twist (the deepest truth):** Veyl is **not dead — it is grieving**, and the Sea is not a
corpse but an open, weeping **wound, still dreaming**. Every crystal is a tear it hasn't finished
crying. The "history" the orders fight over is the god *re-remembering its own life as it dies*.
The **salt-dead rise** because the god is flinching at moments it cannot let go. When the last
pool dries, Veyl finishes the thought — and **whichever version of the past is crystallized at
that instant becomes the only past that ever was.** The orders think they are scavengers looting
a divine cadaver. They are the competing voices inside a dying mind's last sentence. The loser's
past does not just fail to score — **it ceases ever to have happened.**

## Key terms (use these words)

- **The Long Toll** — the shared, audible doomsday countdown of the receding brine; measured in
  **tides** (rounds). Everyone hears the same clock. When it strikes **Dry**, the game ends.
- **Salt-glass / a Litany** — a crystallized memory. A *named*, lore-bearing crystal is a
  **Litany**; common ones are just salt-glass. Fragile, irreplaceable, **humming**.
- **Brine-deep / Dry** — the two states of any zone. **Brine-deep** (wet) **cushions** crystals
  (auto-passes shatter). **Dry** zones make a crystal **Exposed** — and only an Exposed crystal
  can be shattered. On the dying flat zones only ever go **Dry, never wet again.**
- **Exposed** — a crystal in a Dry zone, shatter-eligible. The Iconoclasts' whole hunt is to
  reach the youngest Exposed glass first.
- **The Verdict** — the end scoring: which faction's *version of the truth* survived intact.
  **For an Iconoclast, to "win the Verdict" is to fail** — survival of any past is the disease.
- **Iodine-blind** — slang for someone who has re-lived so many crystals they can no longer tell
  their own memories from the Sea's. The **smell of iodine = a memory crystallizing nearby.**
  For Iconoclasts, blindness is not decay but **promotion**: the blind hear the god undiluted.
- **Reliquiae / the salt-dead** — drowned-grey ghosts of forgotten moments, half-risen from the
  crust, re-remembering how to walk wherever the tide abandons them.
- **The wake** — running through brine makes a wake that shatters glass. The Mnemarchs fear it;
  the Iconoclasts do not care, because for them a shattered crystal is the point.

## The four orders (each *answers the same question*: what is the past FOR?)

- **The Mnemarchs (Order of the Set Hour)** — the past is sacred; **FIX** it forever. Bone-white &
  verdigris bronze; they bunker around a Litany and Set it. *The Iconoclasts' opposite.*
- **The Ash Iconoclasts (the Shattering)** — the past is a wound; **ERASE** it. *Our faction.*
  Charcoal & ember-orange; iodine-fire; mallets, picks and censers; zealots who aim *at* history.
- **The Brine-Factors (the Salt Exchange)** — the past is inventory; **SELL** it. Fast skiff-riders,
  cutpurses, editors of memory. Brass & teal.
- **The Sediment (the Re-Dreaming)** — the past should be **FREED**; the salt-dead swarm, grief
  given a face. They re-dream the dead back into play.

## THE ASH ICONOCLASTS — deep canon

**Battle cry:** *"Let it stop hurting! BREAK IT ALL!"*
**Color:** charcoal & ember-orange — cooling-slag black, the dull glow of iodine-heat, ash-grey
rags, slag-plate; faces scarred with self-cut **crack-lines** mimicking a shattering Litany.
**Identity:** the Shattering. Not nihilists — **anaesthetists**. They believe the kindest thing
one can do to a being trapped re-living its worst moment is to **break the loop**. They smell of
**burnt iodine** constantly (a sweet, acrid reek the other factions fear on the wind); where they
have been, history has a hole no one can remember was ever filled.
**Playstyle (must feel true in the story):** fast, fragile, fearless, suicidal aggression. Cheap
bodies thrown into the youngest Dry to reach brittle glass first; they **aim at the prize, not the
man**; they cannot be demoralized; they **do not carry, hold, or keep** — they unmake and move on.

### Origin — the same hermit, the rest of the story
The first Iconoclast was **the wading hermit of the First Recession** — the *same* hermit the
Mnemarchs canonize as a saint of memory (their Osseil). The Iconoclasts know the rest: after
touching a thousand fossilized moments the hermit went **iodine-blind**, could no longer tell
their own grief from the Sea's, and understood the unbearable truth first — the crystals are not
relics, they are **wounds**, and the god is **not dead, it is screaming**. The hermit took up a
salt-pick and **shattered the very first Litany they had ever set**, and for one clean instant felt
the Sea **go quiet**. That silence is the only scripture the Iconoclasts have, and they have chased
it ever since: **mercy is not preservation but cessation.** Where the other orders crystallized
around what the past is *for*, the Iconoclasts crystallized around the opposite — that the past
should not **be at all**, that "for" is the lie that keeps the god flinching.

### Culture (deliberate forgetting)
A culture of deliberate forgetting, and therefore one with almost **no internal memory** — which
they consider a sacrament, not a flaw. They **keep no records, name no dead**, and burn (in
iodine-fire, the only fire that takes on the wet flat) anything threatening to become a relic.
They speak in the **present tense only** — to use the past tense is a minor heresy, a small act of
letting a moment harden. New initiates perform the **joining rite**: they **shatter the crystal of
their own most precious memory** — the wedding, the child's face, the one good afternoon — and are
told the ache they feel afterward *is the god's ache*, and now they understand the work. Their
faces are scarred with **self-inflicted crack-lines** mimicking a Litany's fracture-pattern: to
look like a broken crystal is to keep faith with what they do.

### Why they fight — to lose
They **fight to lose, and have made peace with it** — which is what makes them terrifying. An
Iconoclast who "won the Verdict" would have failed utterly, because winning means a version of the
past survived, and **survival is the disease.** Their whole war effort is **negation**: when the
last pool dries, let **no ontology be intact** — let Veyl finish its dying thought on a **blank**,
carrying nothing into death but the silence the first hermit heard. A drawn battle where everything
is shattered is, to them, the highest possible outcome — **not a stalemate but a successful exorcism.**

### Leadership & ranks (lived hierarchy — and the succession horror)
- **The Choir of Last Voices** — the council, the **most profoundly iodine-blind** among them; they
  have re-lived so many crystals they have **no sense of which memories are their own**, and so (the
  order believes) hear the god's grief **most clearly, undiluted by self**. They do not give orders
  so much as **point at what must be silenced**; carried on **salt-litters** because most can no
  longer tell which way is down.
- **Cantor-Breakers** — field command: zealots **not yet fully blind**, who retain enough self to
  **aim** and enough sight to lead. A tuning-pick finds a Litany's **fracture-note** by ear.
- **The succession horror:** a Cantor-Breaker who fights well enough, long enough, re-lives enough
  crystals in the breaking to **eventually join the Choir** — meaning **the reward for excellence is
  to lose yourself entirely.** They call this a **promotion**, and march faster for it.

### Homeland — the Scour (a moving condition, not a place)
They **have no homeland and forbid themselves one**, because a home is a place that **remembers
you**. They live in **the Scour** — not a location but a **moving condition**: the band of
freshly-Dried flat directly **behind the receding tide**, where the salt-glass is youngest, most
brittle, and the salt-dead rise thickest. They follow the recession like carrion follow an army,
breaking the just-fossilized before it can set. Camps pitched at dusk-that-never-comes, struck
before the Toll, leaving **no trace but glass-dust and the iodine reek.** Ask one where they are
from and they will say, truthfully, **"from nowhere yet — the place I was born has not been
shattered into not-having-happened. But it will be."**

### Signature mechanic — **Collateral Is the Point** (surface as lived ritual, not rules)
When an Iconoclast shoots an enemy in or beside an **Exposed** crystal's zone, they may declare a
**Breaking Volley**: the shot resolves against the man, but **every die** (hits *and* misses) is
*also* rolled against the crystal, and a natural **5 or 6 shatters** it (where everyone else needs a
6 for stray collateral). The prize must be **Dry/Exposed** (brine still cushions it). Where the
other three orders agonize over *not* breaking the prize, the Iconoclasts **point the whole gun at
history on purpose** — and the guard who came to protect that crystal must decide: **stand in front
of it and eat the volley, or step away and let the Scour have its silence.**

Faction doctrines (texture for the prose):
- **We Keep No Relics** — an Iconoclast can **never Hold and never Set** a crystal. In base contact
  with a Loose crystal they have one verb no one else has: **AUTO-SHATTER** it — no roll, simply
  gone, **it never happened.** A shattered crystal scores **for nobody** — which, for them, *is* the win.
- **Faith Without Memory** — they **auto-pass every morale/panic/rout test; the tests do not exist
  for them.** A culture that refuses the past tense cannot be demoralized by what just happened to
  it; an Iconoclast **does not remember that half the unit is dead.** They stand and die to the last.
- **The Scour Advances** — when a zone flips Brine-deep→**Dry** this tide, Iconoclasts wholly within
  it get a **free move toward the nearest Exposed crystal** — they ride the receding tide like a wave.
- **The Iodine-Fire** — when an Iconoclast destroys a crystal, the empty zone **catches**: anyone
  ending an activation there risks a wound. A shattered Litany becomes a **no-man's-land** the enemy
  cannot comfortably reclaim. On the dying flat (zones never re-flood) **a fire they light stays lit.**

### Named people (use / adapt these; invent a protagonist consistent with them)
- **Vael-Who-Is-Nothing-Yet**, First Cantor of the Cooling-Slag Choir — "the Quiet She Heard." Has
  touched so many crystals she no longer knows her own grief from the Sea's; chases the one clean
  silence she heard the day she shattered her **first** Litany. Once per battle she can will a named
  Exposed crystal to simply go (**"The Sea Goes Quiet"**), the way the first hermit did.
- **Korrun the Match** — a **Last-Voice Penitent** who "burned everything in the joining and came up
  laughing"; shattered his dearest memory at dawn and fights in the **raw open wound** of it, racing
  to make the whole world feel the hole he just put in himself. He spends himself "like a struck
  match" and **will break something before the round ends, even if it ruins the plan.**
- **The Tide-Carrion** (subfaction) — hunt where the Sediment's salt-dead rise thickest; shatter the
  crystal a Reliquia exists to protect and the **dream that held it breaks**, taking the horde with it.
- **The Last-Voice Penitents** (subfaction) — initiates fresh from the joining rite, fighting in the
  un-dulled ache; angriest, fastest, most suicidal — grief itself weaponized.

### Units (lived roles — weave these in as people the recruit knows / becomes)
- **Slag-Pick Penitent** — cheap aggressive line; fresh joining-scars, an iodine-heated salt-pick;
  dies easily and does not care; the expendable wave sent into the Dry to expose prizes with corpses.
- **Scour-Walker** — fast skirmisher on **bare salt-crusted feet**, always in the youngest Dry,
  reaching the most brittle glass first; "travels light because they keep nothing."
- **Iodine Censer-Thrower** — lobs censers that bloom in sweet acrid fire, **useless on armour,
  ruinous on glass**; aims at the Litany, not the guard, and lets the guard choose whether to stand.
- **Cantor-Breaker** — field commander; finds the **fracture-note** by ear; where they sing, the
  glass wants to come apart; knows the reward for fighting well is to **join the Choir and vanish.**
- **Voice of the Last Hour** — a profoundly blind Choir veteran with a great slag-maul; **marches
  toward any silence it can hear** and breaks whatever is loud — friend, foe, or prize alike.
- **Ash-Anchorite** — lone Litany-assassin; fasts and re-lives one chosen crystal until they know
  exactly how it must break, infiltrates alone, **auto-shatters one named Litany**, leaves no trace.
- **Glass-Wraith Detonator** — a penitent who has **packed their own body** with iodine-salt and
  crack-lined glass; closes to a prize and **simply ends**, taking themself, the crystal, and its
  guard out of history in one bloom of sweet fire. "To forget yourself and a Litany in the same instant."
- **Litter of the Choir** — a salt-litter bearing a blind Voice of the Choir, borne by chained
  Penitents who walk where the blind one **points**; it cannot see the battle and does not need to.

### Sensory palette for prose & art
Rust-red never-setting sun over a cracked drying salt-flat; **charcoal and cooling-slag black**;
**ember-orange** iodine-heat glowing in pick-heads and censers; the sweet **acrid iodine reek** and
its medicinal amber haze (here a weapon, not a sacrament); **self-cut crack-line scars** like a
shattered crystal worn on the face; ash-grey rags and slag-plate; **glass-dust** drifting where a
Litany just died; brine teal-green shrinking pools; drowned-grey salt-dead clawing up from the
crust; the **iodine-fire** burning in an emptied zone that the water will never come back to quench.
Mood: feverish, merciful-cruel, suicidally calm, grief turned outward into a hammer — *the relief of
the bell that finally stops ringing.* (Where the Mnemarch film is reverent and hushed, this one is
hotter, harsher, more violent — but still tragic, still tender at its core: every break is an act of love.)
